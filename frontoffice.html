The Auction, Graded

Room Dollars. Each position gets the pot this room actually spent on it at the auction, above the $1 minimums: QB $91 (top $33) · RB $959 (top $69) · WR $1,007 (top $70) · TE $149 (top $33) · K $6 (top $3) · DEF $3 (top $3). That pot is split among every NFL player above the position's starter line (QB12 · RB33 · WR38 · TE14 · K12 · DEF12, from what the league actually starts; today that line sits at 69 at QB, 31 at RB, 40 at WR, 42 at TE, 37 at K, 28 at DEF), drafted or not, with bigger shares the farther above the line he sits, on a curve fitted once to this room's own price ladder. A pick is credited only with points scored while on the drafter's roster, so production nobody drafted, or that somebody cut, takes its share and walks. Nobody can be worth more than the most the room paid at his position. Draft night is the same math on Sleeper's Week 1 projections, with the starred picks' money left out. Grades are on the curve against the room. Tap a card for every pick.

Prices are the auction. Points count only while the player is on the roster that drafted him; once he is cut, his points belong to the Wire tab. Every position counts, and a defense or a kicker can only ever win a share of the few dollars the room put on defenses and kickers. A starred draft-night value means Sleeper had no Week 1 projection for him, so the price paid stands in. Two honest reads worth knowing: a position leader can show a small loss when part of his position's pot has walked to players nobody drafted (this week $15 of the $91 quarterback pot went to quarterbacks nobody drafted), and a pick reads as $1 until his points clear the starter line, however much he cost.
